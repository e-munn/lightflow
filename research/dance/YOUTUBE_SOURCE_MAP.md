Researched September 21, 2026. Goal: audio-only live inference producing one brightness signal, with dance providing examples of expressive timing and phrasing during training.

Start screening **Poppin John SBK and takeSomeCrime**, with Catgroove as the positive reference. The user explicitly asked for more of these and less of viral-trend material such as Pumped Up Kicks. Marquese Scott / Pumped Up Kicks is now deprioritized. The working interpretation is controlled popping, isolation and sustained rhythmic groove; popularity itself is not the criterion. Rankings remain research judgments, not measured clean-clip yields.

Evidence boundary: this pass checked public channel identities, indexed titles/descriptions, creator pages and the existing local source registry. It did not visually audit complete videos. Stable camera, full-body visibility, performer count, real-time motion, tracking quality and audio alignment must be verified per selected passage. A thumbnail or the word “solo” does not certify those properties. No videos were downloaded in this discovery pass.

## Search procedure

Use creator name plus **solo freestyle**, **practice**, **full body**, **one take**, **fixed camera**, **wide shot**, **judge demo**, or **FRONTROW/BACKROW**. They are discovery terms, not guarantees. Prefer original creator/event uploads over compilations and reuploads.

Screen five candidate videos per priority source before expanding it. Record usable uninterrupted seconds rather than total upload duration. Rank sources by clean expressive minutes per hour of review, not subscribers, likes or upload count.

For each candidate:

1. Seek 30–120 seconds of one clearly identifiable dancer, with head, hands and feet visible and a stable background. A stationary audience is acceptable only if subject identity remains reliable. Mirrors still create additional apparent people.
2. Inspect the whole proposed interval for cuts, zooms, camera translation, slow-motion edits, speed ramps, body cropping, occlusion and a change of dancer. Split at edits rather than bridging them with fake smooth motion.
3. Keep musical performance at its original speed with synchronized audio. Remove spoken teaching, countdowns, introductions, applause-only sections and outtakes. Retain source timestamps and record any measured offset.
4. Look for more than activity: controlled stops, intentional stillness, flowing transitions, accents, restraint, and changes of phrase. Do not select only the fastest or most spectacular clips.
5. Confirm the extracted pose preserves the expressive movement. Fine finger choreography may require a future hand-tracking representation; body tracking alone should not label that movement as stillness.
